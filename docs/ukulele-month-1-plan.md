# Ukulele — Month 1

Zero to sing-and-strum in 28 days. 10–15 minutes of learning plus about one
hour of practice per day.

This is **Phase 1 of a possible two-month arc**. Month 1 buys one thing
properly: rhythmic competence in two keys with your voice on top. Month 2 is
not planned yet — it gets written on day 28, from the numbers you log here.
Month 1 still stands alone. Stop at day 28 and you can play and sing five
songs and read a chord sheet cold.

---

## 1. How to use this

### The three rules everything else follows from

**1. Practise the transition, not the chord.**
A chord shape is a two-day skill. The *change* between two shapes under a
steady pulse is a four-week skill. Your unit of practice is the pair — C→Am —
not the chord. Count clean changes in 60 seconds. That number is your
dashboard and it is what month 2 gets designed from.

**2. The strumming hand never stops.**
The loudest beginner tell is the right hand freezing while the left hand hunts
for the next shape. Time is what a listener actually hears. A fluffed chord in
time sounds like a style choice; a perfect chord out of time sounds broken. If
something has to break, the left hand breaks — strum air, strum muted strings,
strum two of the three notes. Never pause the pulse.

**3. Learning is capped at 15 minutes.**
The 10–15 minute block is input: watch, read, understand. The hour is
closed-loop practice with feedback. The classic failure is input eating output.
When the 15 minutes are up you stop consuming and start playing, even mid-video.

### The daily hour

| Block | Min | What |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 5 | Chromatic 1-2-3-4 drill (§4.1), stretches |
| Change drills | 10 | One-minute changes (§4.2), 4–6 pairs, logged |
| Right hand | 10 | Strum pattern on one chord, metronome, left hand idle |
| Song surgery | 15 | Loop the two hard bars (§4.5), not the whole song |
| Integration | 10 | Full play-through, pulse held, mistakes allowed |
| Free + log | 9 | Play for fun, record on day 7/14/21/28, note tomorrow's fix |
| Seed | 1 | Fretboard or ear drill (§4.6), from day 11 |

Split the hour if your day allows — two 30s or three 20s beats one 60. Week 1
fingertips will insist on this anyway.

### The floor

Bad day? Run the 12-minute floor: 3 min warm-up, 4 min change drills, 5 min one
song. It counts. Consistency compounds and heroics do not, and this has to last
eight weeks, not four.

### Days 7, 14, 21, 28

Review, record, and play only. No new material. Rests your fingers and doubles
as a spaced-retrieval day. Record 60 seconds on your phone and watch it back —
you cannot hear your own timing while playing, and this is the highest-leverage
habit in the whole plan.

### Soreness

Dull fingertip soreness is expected in week 1 and gone in 7–10 days. Sharp
pain, wrist pain, or numbness means stop for the day. Most beginners press
three times harder than they need to; see §8.

### Where to get the 15-minute input

Free and good for this specific goal: Cynthia Lin's beginner strumming and
chord lessons, The Ukulele Teacher, Bernadette Teaches Music, Ukulele
Underground. Search the day's topic, watch one video, stop. Do not queue three.

## 2. Setup check — do this once, day 1

**Tuning is gCEA.** From the string closest to your face to the one closest to
the floor: g, C, E, A. The g is **reentrant** — tuned an octave *up*, higher in
pitch than the C next to it. This is why the ukulele sounds bright and jangly
rather than like a small guitar, and it is why the intuition "top string = low
note" does not work here. Do not try to fix it. It is the instrument.

**Tuner.** Clip-on headstock tuner or a phone app (GuitarTuna, Fender Tune).
Check tuning at the start of every single session. New strings drift constantly
for the first week or two — that is normal, not a fault.

**Holding it.** Hold the body against your chest with your right forearm. The
left hand supports nothing — it must be completely free to move. If your left
hand is holding the instrument up, every chord change fights gravity.

**Where to strum.** Over the 12th fret, roughly where the neck meets the body —
*not* over the soundhole. Softer, warmer, and the strings have the most give
there. Strum with the nail side of your index finger going down and the fleshy
pad going up. Thumb alone for quiet passages.

**Strumming motion.** Rotate from the wrist and forearm, not the elbow. Loose.
If your arm gets tired in five minutes you are using the elbow.

**Left thumb.** Flat on the back of the neck, roughly opposite your middle
finger. Not hooked over the top.

## 3. Chord library

Diagrams read left to right as g C E A. Rows are frets 1–4. `o` is a finger.
The four numbers after the name are the fret per string in g-C-E-A order.

```
C   0003          Am  2000          F   2010          G   0232
g C E A           g C E A           g C E A           g C E A
- - - -  1        - - - -  1        - - o -  1        - - - -  1
- - - -  2        o - - -  2        o - - -  2        - o - o  2
- - - o  3        - - - -  3        - - - -  3        - - o -  3
- - - -  4        - - - -  4        - - - -  4        - - - -  4
```

- **C** — ring finger, 3rd fret, A string. The easiest chord on the instrument.
  *Failure:* fingertip flattens and mutes the E string next door.
- **Am** — middle finger, 2nd fret, g string. Also one finger.
  *Failure:* none, really. This is your free chord.
- **F** — index on 1st fret E, middle on 2nd fret g.
  *Failure:* the index leans over and deadens the A string. Arch the knuckle and
  come in more vertically.
- **G** — index 2nd fret C, middle 2nd fret A, ring 3rd fret E. Three fingers in
  a cluster and the hardest of the four.
  *Failure:* cramping. Plant the middle finger on the A string first and let the
  other two fall around it. The cramp is usually thumb pressure, not finger
  strength.

```
Em  0432          Dm  2210          D   2220          A   2100
g C E A           g C E A           g C E A           g C E A
- - - -  1        - - o -  1        - - - -  1        - o - -  1
- - - o  2        o o - -  2        o o o -  2        o - - -  2
- - o -  3        - - - -  3        - - - -  3        - - - -  3
- o - -  4        - - - -  4        - - - -  4        - - - -  4
```

- **Em** — ring 4th fret C, middle 3rd fret E, index 2nd fret A. Awkward angle.
  *Failure:* fingers stack up and mute each other. Come in steeper.
- **Dm** — middle 2nd fret g, ring 2nd fret C, index 1st fret E.
- **D** — three fingers across the 2nd fret on g, C, E. Cramped on a soprano.
  *Alternative:* barre those three strings with your index finger.
- **A** — middle 2nd fret g, index 1st fret C.

```
G7  0212          C7  0001          D7  2223          A7  0100
g C E A           g C E A           g C E A           g C E A
- - o -  1        - - - o  1        - - - -  1        - o - -  1
- o - o  2        - - - -  2        o o o -  2        - - - -  2
- - - -  3        - - - -  3        - - - o  3        - - - -  3
- - - -  4        - - - -  4        - - - -  4        - - - -  4
```

The 7ths are cheap colour. C7 and A7 are one finger each. G7 is easier than G.
D7 also works as `2020` (the "Hawaiian" shape) if `2223` cramps.

## 4. Drill library

Written once here. The daily entries just name them.

### 4.1 Chromatic warm-up (5 min)

One finger per fret: index=1, middle=2, ring=3, pinky=4. Play frets 1-2-3-4 on
the g string, then the C, then E, then A, then back down. One note per
metronome click at 60bpm. Keep every finger you have already placed *down* on
the way up. This builds finger independence, which is the actual physical
bottleneck behind the G chord.

### 4.2 One-minute changes (10 min)

Pick two chords. Set a timer for 60 seconds. Switch back and forth as many
times as you can, counting each complete change. Both directions count. Only
count it if all the strings ring — a muted change is not a change.

Write the number in the log. This is the single most honest measure of progress
you have, and it is what the day-28 decision is made from.

Rough benchmarks for a total beginner:

| Pair | Day 2 | End week 1 | End week 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| C ↔ Am | 10–20 | 40 | 60+ |
| C ↔ F | — | 25 | 50+ |
| Am ↔ G | — | 20 | 45+ |
| G ↔ C | — | 15 | 40+ |

Do not chase the number by cheating the quality. A slow clean change beats a
fast dead one, every time.

### 4.3 Ghost strumming

Keep the strumming hand moving on the full eighth-note grid, down-up-down-up,
continuously — but only make contact on the beats the pattern calls for. The
missed strums are *misses*, not stops. The hand never leaves the groove.

This is the mechanical fix for rule 2 and it is the whole reason the island
strum works.

### 4.4 Metronome ladder

Start at a tempo where you are perfect, not a tempo that feels respectable.
Three clean passes in a row → up 4bpm. Two failed passes → down 8bpm. Never
practise at a tempo where you make errors; you are only rehearsing the error.

### 4.5 Song surgery (15 min)

Do not play songs from the top over and over. Find the two bars where it breaks.
Loop *those two bars plus one bar either side*, slow enough to be clean, ten
clean repetitions in a row. Then play the section around them. Then the song.

Most people spend fifteen minutes replaying the parts they can already play.

### 4.6 Seed drills (1 min, from day 11)

Cheap now, valuable in month 2. Alternate days:

- **Fretboard naming.** Say the note names up the C string: C, C#, D, D#, E, F
  up to the 5th fret. Just say them while touching them.
- **Ear guessing.** Play any recorded song and tap the moment the chord changes.
  You are not naming the chord, only feeling where change happens.

## 5. Strum patterns

Everything sits on the eighth-note grid. Count out loud: `1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &`.
Your hand moves **down on every number, up on every &**, always, forever. The
pattern is only about which of those movements touch the strings.

**Pattern 1 — quarter downs** (week 1)
```
count:  1  &  2  &  3  &  4  &
hand:   D  u  D  u  D  u  D  u
hit:    D     D     D     D
```

**Pattern 2 — straight eighths** (day 8)
```
hand:   D  U  D  U  D  U  D  U
hit:    D  U  D  U  D  U  D  U
```

**Pattern 3 — the island strum, D DU UDU** (day 9 onward — the single highest
value rhythm on this instrument)
```
count:  1  &  2  &  3  &  4  &
hand:   D  u  D  U  d  U  D  U
hit:    D     D  U     U  D  U
```
Lowercase = the hand moves but misses the strings. The miss on beat 3 is what
makes the pattern swing. If you *stop* there instead of missing, it dies.

**Pattern 4 — island with chunks** (day 22)
Same as pattern 3, but on beats 2 and 4 the heel of your strumming hand slaps
the strings as you come down — a percussive click instead of a chord. Now you
have a drummer.

## 6. The 28 days

Each day: the learning topic, the practice focus, the pairs to drill, the target
tempo, and the song.

### Week 1 — Pulse and the four chords

**Day 1** · Learn: gCEA and reentrant high G; how to hold it; where to strum.
Practice: tune it. Down strums on open strings at 60bpm for ten full minutes —
this is not filler, it is the pulse. Then the C chord, four down strums per bar,
checking every string rings. Pairs: none. Song: none.

**Day 2** · Learn: fretting cleanly — fingertip just behind the fret wire,
minimum pressure. Practice: C, then add Am. **Run C↔Am one-minute changes and
write down the number — this is your baseline.** Tempo 60. Song: a C–Am vamp,
two bars each, sing anything over it.

**Day 3** · Learn: the chromatic warm-up (§4.1) and why finger independence is
the real bottleneck. Practice: add F. Pairs: C↔Am, Am↔F, C↔F. Tempo 60. Song:
C–Am–F–Am vamp.

**Day 4** · Learn: the metronome ladder (§4.4). Practice: three chords on the
ladder. Pairs: C↔F, Am↔F. Tempo: wherever you are clean. Song: You Are My
Sunshine using only C and F.

**Day 5** · Learn: the G chord and the anchor-finger trick. Practice: G shape
alone until it rings, then in motion. Pairs: Am↔G, G↔C. Tempo 60. Song: Riptide
chorus — Am, G, C, four down strums each.

**Day 6** · Learn: ghost strumming (§4.3) — the hand never stops. Practice: all
six pairs with ghost strumming through every change. Tempo 60–65. Song: Riptide
verse, Am–G–C, no stopping, however ugly.

**Day 7** · Review, record, no new material. **Week 1 gate:** C↔Am at 40
changes/min, and Riptide played through with the strum hand never stopping.
Record 60 seconds and watch it back — watch the right hand, not the left.

### Week 2 — The island strum

**Day 8** · Learn: the eighth-note grid; counting `1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &` out loud.
Practice: straight eighths (pattern 2) on one chord, left hand idle, 60bpm, the
full ten minutes. Pairs: C↔G, G↔Am. Song: Riptide with straight eighths.

**Day 9** · Learn: the island strum, D DU UDU (pattern 3). Practice: island on
C alone. Nothing else. Right hand only today. Tempo 60. Pairs: skip if time is
short. Song: none — the pattern is the song today.

**Day 10** · Learn: why the beat-3 miss is a miss, not a stop. Practice: island
on each of C, Am, F, G in isolation, one chord at a time. Tempo 60–65. Pairs:
C↔F, Am↔G. Song: Riptide, island strum, one chord per bar.

**Day 11** · Learn: I–V–vi–IV — C, G, Am, F — and why it plays half of pop
music. Practice: pairs C↔G, G↔Am, Am↔F, F↔C. Tempo 65. Song: the four-chord
loop with the island strum. **Seed drill starts** (§4.6).

**Day 12** · Learn: the move-early rule — start changing on beat 4 and accept
the last eighth being muted. Practice: every pair with the early move. Tempo 70.
Song: Three Little Birds (C, F, G), island strum.

**Day 13** · Learn: song surgery (§4.5). Practice: find your two worst bars in
Three Little Birds and loop them until ten clean in a row. Tempo: whatever is
clean. Song: Three Little Birds full, then Riptide.

**Day 14** · Review, record, no new material. **Week 2 gate:** island strum at
80bpm through a full four-chord song with the hand never stopping, and three
songs playable start to finish. Record and watch.

### Week 3 — Voice and the key of G

**Day 15** · Learn: why singing and strumming collide, and the four stages —
autopilot strum, hum, chorus words, full song. Practice: island strum while
counting 1-2-3-4 out loud. If you cannot talk over it, it is not automatic yet.
Tempo 75. Song: Three Little Birds, humming the melody over it.

**Day 16** · Learn: chord families — the handful of chords that belong to a key,
and why songs stay inside them. Practice: pairs, all four C-key chords. Tempo
80. Song: Three Little Birds, chorus words sung, verses hummed.

**Day 17** · Learn: the key of G — G, C, D, Em. New shapes: D and Em. Practice:
the two new shapes until they ring. Pairs: G↔D, G↔Em, Em↔C. Tempo 60 for the new
pairs. Song: Country Roads (G, Em, D, C), down strums only.

**Day 18** · Learn: transposing — the same I–V–vi–IV shape moved from C to G.
Practice: play the four-chord loop in C, then in G, back to back, same strum.
Tempo 70. Song: Country Roads with the island strum.

**Day 19** · Learn: phrasing — where you actually breathe in a verse, and why
the strum does not wait for you. Practice: pairs in G. Tempo 80. Song: Stand By
Me (C, Am, F, G), chorus sung.

**Day 20** · Learn: the phone-number test — if you cannot recite your phone
number while strumming, the pattern is not automatic. Practice: strum while
talking, deliberately. Tempo 80. Song: your best song, sung all the way through,
badly, twice.

**Day 21** · Review, record, no new material. **Week 3 gate:** one full song
sung and strummed start to finish, recorded. Watch it back and note one thing
to fix.

### Week 4 — Feel, consolidation, decision

**Day 22** · Learn: the chunk — the palm slap on beats 2 and 4 (pattern 4).
Practice: chunks on one chord until the click is crisp. Tempo 75. Song: Three
Little Birds with chunks.

**Day 23** · Learn: dynamics as a decision — quiet verse, loud chorus, chosen
rather than accidental. Practice: the same eight bars at two deliberate volumes.
Tempo 80. Song: Stand By Me with a genuinely quiet verse.

**Day 24** · Learn: the cheap 7ths — G7, C7, D7 — and what they do to a
turnaround. Practice: substitute G7 for G everywhere; drop C7 in before an F.
Pairs: C↔C7, G7↔C. Tempo 80. Song: You Are My Sunshine with C7 on the turnaround.

**Day 25** · Learn: Em and Dm inside a real song rather than as shapes.
Practice: pairs C↔Em, Am↔Dm, F↔G. Tempo 70. Song: Can't Help Falling in Love
(C, Em, Am, F, G, Dm), slow.

**Day 26** · **Cold-read test.** No learning block today. Pick a song you have
never played, in C or G, pull up its chord sheet, and play it through twice with
a metronome. Log honestly how it went — that result is the real measure of the
month. Then normal song work on the repertoire.

**Day 27** · Repertoire lock. No new material. Five songs, start to finish,
back to back, no stopping between them. That is a set. Play it twice.

**Day 28** · **Performance and decision.** Record three songs, singing at least
two. Watch them back. Then fill in §9 and decide about month 2.

## 7. Weekly gates

Pass or fail, no interpretation.

| | Test | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 1** | C↔Am one-minute changes | 40+ clean changes |
| | Riptide played through | strum hand never stopped |
| **Week 2** | Island strum, four-chord song | held at 80bpm, no stops |
| | Repertoire | 3 songs start to finish |
| **Week 3** | One song sung and strummed | complete, recorded, no restart |
| | Key of G | four-chord loop played in both C and G |
| **Week 4** | Performance | 3 songs played, 2 sung |
| | Cold read (day 26) | unfamiliar sheet played in time by pass 2 |

Missing a gate is information, not failure. It tells you what month 2 starts on.

## 8. Troubleshooting

**Buzzing strings.** Your fingertip is too far behind the fret. Move it right up
against the fret wire, on the tip, not the pad. Check your nails — if they touch
the fretboard before your fingertip does, cut them.

**The F chord kills the A string.** Your index finger is leaning over from the E
string. Arch the knuckle and come in more vertically. Push your wrist slightly
forward, away from the neck.

**The G chord cramps.** Almost always thumb pressure, not finger weakness. Plant
the middle finger on the 2nd fret of the A string first and let the other two
fall into place around it. Then consciously relax the thumb until the chord
starts to buzz, and back off from there — that is your actual minimum pressure,
and it is far less than you were using.

**Dead or dull chord.** Either not enough pressure, or a finger is brushing an
adjacent string. Pick each string individually and find which one is dead. The
culprit is nearly always the neighbouring finger.

**You lose time during changes.** You are waiting for the chord to be ready. Fix
with ghost strumming (§4.3) and the move-early rule from day 12: start moving on
beat 4 and let the last eighth be muted. A muted eighth is invisible. A pause is
not.

**Fingertips hurt.** Normal for 7–10 days. Split practice into shorter blocks and
use the minimum-pressure test above — most of the pain is from pressing three
times harder than necessary.

**Singing and strumming fight each other.** The strum is not automatic yet. Go
back to the phone-number test on day 20. Do not push through by concentrating
harder; concentration is exactly the resource that runs out.

**You cannot remember the shapes.** You are recalling them visually, which does
not survive tempo. The one-minute change drills are the fix — they build motor
memory, which does.

## 10. The day-28 gate

Month 2 is earned, not assumed. Three criteria, judged from the log above.

- **Consistency** — 20 of 28 days practised at floor level or above.
- **Skill** — all four weekly gates passed, or three of four with the fourth close.
- **Appetite** — you still pick the thing up unprompted.

**All three hold** → month 2 gets designed from your logged numbers. Which of
fingerpicking, movable shapes, ear training, or repertoire depth comes first
depends on what the log says is weak, which is why it is not decided today.

**Consistency failed, appetite held** → month 2 is this same material at a lower
daily budget. Twenty minutes a day that actually happens beats an hour that does
not.

**Appetite failed** → that is real information and worth saying plainly. Either
the songs were wrong (pick music you actually love, the spine here is chosen for
chord difficulty, not taste), or an hour a day is the wrong shape for you.
Neither means you cannot play the ukulele.

## 11. Month 2 preview

Everything below was **deliberately excluded** from month 1. Naming the
deferrals is what kept month 1 fast — a visible deferral is a decision, an
invisible one is just a gap you feel guilty about.

**Not yet, in month 1:** fingerpicking · barre and movable chord shapes · the E
chord · keys beyond C and G · standard notation · theory past chord families ·
playing with other people.

**Likely month 2 themes**, in rough order of payoff:

1. **Fingerpicking** — the standard patterns, then a picked arrangement. Turns
   you from an accompanist into someone who can play alone.
2. **Movable shapes and the barre** — one shape, twelve chords. This is the
   single biggest unlock on the fretboard and the reason the seed drill has you
   naming notes now.
3. **Ear and transposition** — hearing a change coming, finding a key by ear,
   playing along with a recording cold.
4. **Repertoire depth and performance** — a fifteen-minute set played for actual
   humans, which is a different skill from playing well alone.

The real month-2 plan gets written on day 28 against your log, not guessed at
now. Whichever of these your numbers say is weakest goes first.
